# Colour Philosophy

The colour system represents the connection between humans and technology.

The portfolio should feel like a calm creative environment where research, empathy, creativity, and digital design come together.

The colour palette is inspired by:

- Nature
- Japanese minimalism
- Creative workspaces
- Designer notebooks
- Calm coffee shop environments
- Thoughtful digital products

The goal is to create a balance between:

**Human warmth + Digital creativity**

The colours should feel comforting and memorable while remaining professional and suitable for a UX/UI Designer portfolio.

---

# Colour Direction

## Overall Concept

**Nature + Technology**

The palette combines:

- Sage green
- Lavender purple
- Warm brown neutrals
- Soft cream backgrounds

Together these colours communicate:

- Calmness
- Growth
- Creativity
- Trust
- Curiosity
- Human-centred thinking

The website should feel like:

"A calm designer workspace where ideas, research, and technology meet."

---

# Colour Meaning

## Sage Green 🌿

### Purpose

Represents the human side of design.

### Meaning

- Empathy
- Balance
- Growth
- Accessibility
- User-centred thinking
- Understanding people

### Usage

Sage should be used for:

- UX process elements
- Journey map sections
- Research indicators
- Supporting highlights
- Success states
- Subtle background areas

Sage represents the connection between design and people.

---

## Lavender Purple 💜

### Purpose

Represents creativity and digital design.

### Meaning

- Curiosity
- Innovation
- Imagination
- Exploration
- Creative problem solving

### Usage

Purple should be used for:

- Primary interactive elements
- Buttons
- Important highlights
- Links
- Creative details

Purple represents the creative side of UX/UI design.

---

## Warm Brown 🤎

### Purpose

Adds warmth and personality.

### Meaning

- Craftsmanship
- Trust
- Comfort
- Human connection
- Personal touch

### Usage

Brown should be used carefully for:

- Small decorative elements
- Notebook-inspired details
- Annotations
- Supporting visual elements

Brown should never dominate the interface.

---

## Warm Neutral Backgrounds 🤍

### Purpose

Create a calm workspace feeling.

The background should feel similar to:

- Paper
- A designer notebook
- A creative studio environment

Usage:

- Main page background
- Sections
- Content areas
- Large surfaces

Avoid pure white as the main background because it can feel too sterile.

---

# Colour Balance Rules

The colour hierarchy should follow:

## 70% — Warm Neutrals

Used for:

- Backgrounds
- Large sections
- Reading areas

Creates calmness and allows content to breathe.

---

## 20% — Sage Green

Used for:

- Supporting elements
- UX process visuals
- Journey sections
- Secondary highlights

Creates the human-centred identity.

---

## 10% — Lavender Purple

Used for:

- Primary actions
- Interactive states
- Important moments

Creates creativity and memorability.

---

# Colour Usage Principles

Colours should guide attention, not decorate unnecessarily.

Use colour intentionally for:

- Buttons
- Navigation states
- Interactive elements
- UX process indicators
- Important information

Avoid:

- Large blocks of saturated colour
- Excessive gradients
- Multiple competing accent colours
- Overly pastel designs
- Neon colours

---

# Colour Mood

The final feeling should be:

- Calm
- Creative
- Natural
- Professional
- Welcoming

The portfolio should feel:

"A thoughtful UX designer's workspace where nature-inspired colours meet modern technology."

---

# Avoid

Do not use:

- Neon purple
- Extremely saturated colours
- Cold corporate blue palettes
- Dark luxury themes
- Washed-out colours with no personality
- Trend-based colours that reduce timelessness

The colour system should feel personal, intentional, and connected to my identity as a UX/UI Designer.

# Typography Philosophy

Typography represents the balance between human creativity and digital clarity.

The portfolio should combine personality with usability.

Typography should feel:

- Elegant
- Thoughtful
- Modern
- Professional
- Human

The goal is to create a reading experience similar to a well-designed editorial publication while maintaining the clarity required for digital products.

---

# Typography Direction

The typography system should combine:

## Display Typography

Used for:

- Hero headings
- Main page titles
- Important statements
- Section introductions

Purpose:

Create personality and emotional connection.

The display font should feel:

- Creative
- Refined
- Memorable
- Human

A subtle serif style can be used to create warmth and craftsmanship.

---

## Body Typography

Used for:

- Paragraphs
- Navigation
- Case studies
- Descriptions
- Supporting information

Purpose:

Prioritise readability and accessibility.

The body font should feel:

- Clean
- Modern
- Simple
- Digital

A sans-serif style is preferred.

---

# Typography Relationship

The combination should represent:

Human side:

Elegant serif typography

+

Digital side:

Clean sans-serif typography

Together they communicate:

"Thoughtful human-centred design supported by modern technology."

---

# Font Rules

Typography should maintain:

- Clear hierarchy
- Comfortable line spacing
- Strong readability
- Consistent usage

Avoid:

- Using too many fonts
- Decorative fonts for body text
- Difficult-to-read typefaces
- Trendy fonts that reduce professionalism

---

# Heading Style

Headings should:

- Feel confident
- Create visual impact
- Have enough breathing space
- Guide the user's attention

Large headings should not overwhelm the page.

---

# Body Text Style

Body text should:

- Be easy to scan
- Use comfortable line length
- Maintain strong contrast
- Support storytelling

UX case studies should prioritise reading comfort.

---

# Typography Hierarchy

The website should maintain a clear structure:

## H1

Purpose:

Main message of each page.

Should feel unique and memorable.

---

## H2

Purpose:

Major sections.

Should create clear organisation.

---

## H3

Purpose:

Supporting information and smaller sections.

---

## Body

Purpose:

Detailed explanations.

Should always prioritise readability.

---

# Typography Personality

The final typography feeling should be:

"A designer's editorial notebook transformed into a modern digital experience."

Typography should support the story of the portfolio without becoming the focus itself.
